ANSWER KEY  ·  Quiz 11 · Unit 9 (pages 125–133)20 points total

Part A · Multiple Choice 2 points each

  1. a) Earth’s internal heat engine and the hydrological cycle
    p. 126 — the internal heat engine moves material through the core and mantle; the hydrological cycle moves water, ice, and air at the surface and is powered by the Sun.
  2. d) Its core is no longer hot enough to drive mantle convection, and it has no atmosphere or liquid water
    p. 126 — Earth’s rock cycle stays active precisely because our core is still hot, our atmosphere is relatively thick, and we have liquid water.
  3. a) intrusive or plutonic
    pp. 127–128 — slow cooling gives crystals a long time to grow, so intrusive rocks have relatively large, easily visible mineral crystals. Granite is the most common.
  4. b) a batholith
    p. 129 — smaller than 100 km² and it is a stock. Batholiths typically form when a number of stocks coalesce beneath the surface.
  5. b) a sill
    p. 129 — a dyke is discordant, cutting across the layering. Note the distinction is about the relationship to layering, not orientation: a sill can be vertical if the bedding is vertical.
  6. c) Pumice
    p. 130 — obsidian is the volcanic glass that forms when lava cools so fast that no crystals develop at all.

Part C · Short Answer 3 points each

  1. Phaneritic means crystals large enough to see, so cooling was slow. Aphanitic means crystals too small to distinguish, so cooling was fast. Porphyritic means two crystal sizes, so the rock cooled in two stages.
    Rubric: 1 pt each. Phaneritic — crystals visible to the naked eye, larger than about 0.5 mm; typical of intrusive rocks that cooled slowly at depth. Aphanitic — crystals too small to distinguish; typical of volcanic rocks that cooled quickly at the surface. Porphyritic — large crystals set in a finer groundmass, produced by a two-stage cooling process (slow at depth, then rapid after eruption or shallow emplacement). (p. 130)
  2. Olivine, pyroxene, and calcium-rich plagioclase. Cooling slowly underground produces gabbro; cooling quickly at the surface produces basalt.
    Rubric: 1.5 pts for the minerals — silica combines with iron and magnesium to make olivine, then with calcium to make calcium-rich plagioclase, and any leftover silica converts some olivine to pyroxene. Award full credit for naming all three. 1 pt gabbro (slow, underground). 0.5 pt basalt (fast, at the surface). Note for grading: the key insight is that identical magma yields different rocks based on cooling rate alone, while the mineral content stays the same. (pp. 131–132)
