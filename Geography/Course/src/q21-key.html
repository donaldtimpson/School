Part C · Short Answer 3 points each

  1. Porosity is the amount of open pore space between grains — how much water the rock can hold. Permeability is whether those pores connect — whether the water can travel through. An aquifer needs both: space to store water and connections that let it move to a well.
    Rubric: 1 pt porosity defined — the small spaces between sediment grains where water droplets sit. 1 pt permeability defined — the connections between those pores, allowing water to travel through the rock. 1 pt why both are required — a highly porous rock with unconnected pores holds water but cannot yield it, and connections are useless without space to store water in the first place. Credit noting that the impermeable layer beneath is what forms the base of the aquifer. (p. 258)
  2. The water table falls, forcing wells to be dug deeper or leaving them dry; the ground surface may subside beneath houses and structures; and in coastal areas seawater may enter and contaminate the aquifer.
    Rubric: 1.5 pts each for any two. Falling water table — wells must be dug deeper at greater cost and energy, and shallower wells go completely dry. Subsidence — lowering the water table can cause the ground surface to sink, including beneath houses and other structures. Saltwater incursion — in coastal regions seawater enters the depleted aquifer and contaminates it for drinking and irrigation. (p. 259)
