ANSWER KEY  ·  Quiz 2 · Unit 2 (pages 22–38)20 points total

Part A · Multiple Choice 2 points each

  1. a) the galaxy is moving away from Earth
    p. 24 — redshift occurs when the light source moves away from the observer, or when the space between them stretches. Because all galaxies show redshift, the universe must be expanding.
  2. d) the farther away a galaxy is, the faster it is moving away from us
    p. 24 — Hubble combined distance measurements with redshift data and found this relationship, which leads to the hypothesis that the universe is expanding.
  3. b) 13.7 billion years ago
    p. 25 — 4.6 billion years is the age of the solar system, not the universe. 380,000 years is how long after the Big Bang the first neutral atoms formed.
  4. c) nuclear fusion
    p. 33 — in the core of a star, pressure is great enough that two hydrogen atoms fuse to become a helium atom.
  5. a) globular cluster
    p. 29 — open clusters, by contrast, hold up to a few thousand young bluish stars loosely, and still contain glowing gas and dust.
  6. b) 26,000 light-years
    p. 32 — a little more than halfway out from the center to the edge. The disk itself is about 100,000 light-years across.

Part B · True or False 1 point each

  1. TRUE
    p. 30 — most elliptical galaxies also contain very little gas and dust, because it has already formed into stars.
  2. FALSE
    The Milky Way is a typical spiral galaxy, with a disk, a central bulge, and spiral arms. (p. 31)

Part C · Short Answer 3 points each

  1. Dark matter accounts for missing mass; dark energy accounts for the accelerating expansion of the universe.
    Rubric: 1.5 pts each. Dark matter: there is far too little visible matter to make up the roughly 27% of the universe that observations require, so dark matter is the name for that unseen mass. Scientists are more certain what it is not — not stars and planets, not baryonic clouds, not antimatter, not galaxy-sized black holes. Dark energy: a hypothetical form of energy, possibly as much as 72% of the universe’s total energy content, proposed to explain why the rate of expansion is increasing rather than slowing. (pp. 26–28)
  2. Fusion stops completely, and gravitational collapse shrinks the star’s core into a white dwarf.
    Rubric: 1 pt fusion stops; 1 pt gravitational collapse of the core; 1 pt the result is a white dwarf — a white, glowing object about the size of Earth — which eventually fades out. Award credit if the student also describes the earlier red giant stage (outer layers grow outward and cool, turning the star red). Do not give credit for supernova, neutron star, or black hole — those require a much more massive star. (p. 36)
